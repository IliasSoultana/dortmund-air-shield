"Physics keeps the link alive — but how will tomorrow's smart attackers try to bypass standard threat detectors? (1s pause)

Instead of turning a jammer on at full power all at once, a smart attacker increases jamming power very slowly (+1.55 dB/s) — a Ramping Jammer. Why does that fool standard threshold rules for 9.5 seconds? Because a standard detector only compares the current signal to the moving average of the last 4 seconds — when jamming increases slowly, that 4-second average shifts down right along with the attack, just like your eyes adjusting to a slowly darkening room!

Our Contrastive Prototype Detector catches it at 0.0 seconds: at takeoff, it freezes a healthy baseline of 4 sensors (Signal Strength, Signal Clarity, Packet Loss, and GPS-to-INS offset). Because that takeoff baseline never shifts, it catches slow jamming and slow GPS drift at 0.0s with 0 false flags! And when a novel combined attack hits, it stays honest (MPI-SP principle) and flags Unexplained instead of guessing."
