"That brings us to the bottom half of the right sidebar — our Threat Intelligence and LLM Security Analyst.

When I worked on anomaly detection during my internship at Fraunhofer, one question always fascinated me: how will tomorrow's smart attackers bypass standard detectors? (1s pause)

Instead of turning a jammer on at full power all at once, a smart attacker increases the jamming power very slowly over time — a Ramping Jammer. Why does that fool a standard detector? Because a standard detector only compares the current signal to the moving average of the last 4 seconds. When the jamming increases slowly, that 4-second average shifts down right along with the attack — just like your eyes adjusting to a slowly darkening room — so the detector stays completely blind for 9.5 seconds (over 130 meters of blind flight).

Our Contrastive Detector fixes this: at takeoff, it saves a frozen healthy baseline of 4 values (Signal Strength, Signal Clarity, Packet Loss, and GPS-to-INS offset). Because that takeoff baseline never shifts, we detect even a slow ramping jammer at 0.0 seconds!

Now, what if an attacker combines two weapons at once — Jamming AND GPS Spoofing — in a pattern we have never seen before? (1s pause) Most AI models blindly guess — and a wrong guess triggers the wrong defense. From my security research discussions at MPI-SP, real trust comes when AI is honest about what it does NOT know. Therefore, instead of guessing, our detector flags DR-03 and DR-07 in yellow as UNEXPLAINED — needs label.

And because this new attack needs a label, that is exactly where our LLM Security Analyst right below comes in! We never let an LLM fly the drone — survival happens automatically in milliseconds, and the LLM runs offline at HQ strictly as a CoPilot for the human commander: it translates those raw sensor jumps into two plain sentences, opens a critical ticket for the ground team, and gives me this Confirm Threat button.

Watch what happens when I click Confirm Threat once: this triggers Collaborative Fleet Learning! HQ takes that tiny 4-number threat fingerprint from DR-03 and DR-07 and broadcasts it to all 10 drones in the fleet — so DR-03 and DR-07 immediately turn blue, and any other drone approaching that sector later immediately recognizes the attack at 0.0 seconds and proactively switches to its DECT backup route and INS lock before its connection can break — keeping our Recovery Time under 3 seconds!

And why is this a game-changer for clients like Morpheus Logistik? Because when one drone learns a new threat, the other nine drones don't have to abort their flights or get grounded for manual inspection — drastically cutting fleet downtime, lowering operational costs, and keeping hospital deliveries on schedule!"

Technical Deep-Dive Notes (For Jury Q&A Only)

How Z-Score, Cosine Similarity, Detector-to-LLM JSON, and Hardware Scope work under the hood

Q&A Ready
1. How Z-Score (>= 4.5) and Cosine Similarity (>= 0.84) Work Under the Hood

• Step 1 — Anomaly Detection (Z-Score): During the 40s (80-tick) takeoff warmup, each drone records the mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) of 4 sensors: [RSSI, SNR, Packet_Loss, GNSS_INS_Offset]. Every 0.5s tick, we compute the 4D Z-score deviation vector [z_rssi, z_snr, z_loss, z_gnss]. If its Euclidean length z_score >= 4.5 (e.g., z = 24.6), an attack is detected at 0.0s.
• Step 2 — Attribution (Cosine Similarity): We compare the direction of that 4D Z-vector against known attack prototypes using Cosine Similarity (sim from 0.00 to 1.00). Pure jamming points along [High, High, Med, 0]; pure spoofing points along [0, 0, 0, High]. When both hit together (Coordinated RF+GNSS), the vector points diagonally between them: max similarity is only sim = 0.68 < 0.84 threshold → flagged as UNEXPLAINED.

2. How the Detector Feeds the LLM & How 1-Click Collaborative Learning Works

• Detector → LLM JSON: The detector passes a structured JSON payload {device: "DR-07", label: "UNEXPLAINED", z_score: 24.6, dims: {rssi: 8.1, snr: 18.2, loss: 4.0, gnss_offset_m: 16.4}, similarity: 0.68} to the LLM Analyst (llm_analyst.py), which runs offline at HQ (with optional live API mode to gpt-6-studierende on chat.kiconnect.nrw/v1/responses).
• 1-Click Fleet Learning (CONFIRM_THREAT): Clicking Confirm Threat normalizes the 4D Z-vector into a unit vector (4 floats = 16 bytes) and saves it in the shared fleet prototype table as coordinated_rf_gnss. On the very next tick, similarity jumps from 0.68 to 0.99 >= 0.84 across all 10 drones.

3. Why Only 4 Sensors in the Detector? & How Do Moving Drones Aim Lasers?

• Why we excluded Latency & Velocity from the 4D detector (Invariance): When a drone flies behind the U-Tower and switches from FSOC Laser to DECT radio, its latency jumps from 5ms to 40ms. If latency or speed were inside the cyberattack detector, every building in Dortmund would trigger a false cyberattack alarm! Keeping only link-budget & navigation residuals gives us 0 false alarms across 1,200 noisy drone-ticks.
• How drones aim FSOC lasers (PAT) & Hardware Economics: Drones broadcast their 3D GPS/INS coordinates over omnidirectional DECT NR+ (`Inter-Drone` channel) so gimbals know where to aim the laser. And because optical heads weigh ~400g and cost more than €15 DECT NR+ chips, our architecture puts high-power FSOC relays on DR-01 and DR-02 at 120m altitude while allowing lower drones to use DECT NR+ seamlessly.
